DOS, PDOS and COHP

Hamster can compute the density of states (DOS), the projected DOS (PDOS) and crystal orbital Hamilton populations (COHP) as a post-processing step of a standard run. The quantities are computed from the real-space Hamiltonian of every structure, so no additional input is needed.

Usage

Add the dos tag to the Options block:

begin Options
    dos = pdos cohp      # any combination of dos, pdos and cohp; dos = all computes all three
end

All other settings are optional and go into a DOS block (see DOS tags), for example:

begin DOS
    projection = element    # element (Pb, Br) or orbital (Pb-s, Pb-px, ...)
    cohp_pairs = Pb-Br      # default: all coupled pairs
    nelec = 8               # electrons per structure (here: GaAs primitive cell): Fermi energy, IDOS and ICOHP
    sigma = 0.05            # broadening (eV) of the exact method
end

The k-points are taken from the kpoints tag of the run (equal weights); for large supercells, Γ (kpoints = gamma, the default) is usually sufficient.

Methods

  • method = exact diagonalizes the Hamiltonian at every k-point and broadens every eigenvalue with a Gaussian or Lorentzian of width sigma. The Fermi energy and the ICOHP are obtained from the eigenvalues and the occupied states directly.
  • method = kpm uses the kernel polynomial method: the DOS, PDOS and COHP are expanded in kpm_moments Chebyshev polynomials (with the Jackson kernel), whose coefficients are estimated with kpm_vectors random-phase vectors. Only sparse matrix-vector products are needed, so this works for supercells with 10⁵ orbitals and more. The energy resolution is about π × bandwidth / (2 × kpm_moments), and the relative stochastic error about 1/√(orbitals × kpm_vectors).
  • method = auto (default) uses the exact method up to exact_max_orbitals orbitals (default 4000) and KPM above.

Conventions

  • Spin: without SOC, all quantities contain the spin degeneracy 2. With SOC (soc = true), every orbital is a spin orbital (degeneracy 1), and the PDOS of the two spin components of an orbital is summed. In both cases, $\int \mathrm{DOS}(E)\, dE$ equals the number of electrons the bands can hold.
  • PDOS: $\mathrm{PDOS}_A(E) = g \sum_{k} w_k \sum_n \delta(E - E_{nk}) \sum_{i \in A} |c_{i n k}|^2$, where $A$ is an element or an orbital type, depending on projection. The sum over all labels gives the DOS.
  • COHP: for a pair of labels $(A, B)$,

    \[\mathrm{COHP}_{AB}(E) = g \sum_k w_k \sum_n \delta(E - E_{nk}) \sum_{i \in A, j \in B \text{ or } i \in B, j \in A} \mathrm{Re}\left(c^*_{ink} H_{ij}(k)\, c_{jnk}\right),\]

    summed over orbitals on different atoms; on-site terms are excluded. Pairs of the same label (e.g. Br-Br) contain the bonds between different atoms of that element. Negative values are bonding, positive values antibonding (plot -COHP to get the common "bonding to the right" picture). The COHP of all pairs plus the on-site contributions equals $E \cdot \mathrm{DOS}(E)$.
  • ICOHP: with nelec, the integrated COHP up to the Fermi energy is written for every pair, together with the integrated curves.

Output

The results are written to dos.h5 (tag filename of the DOS block), with one group per structure (named by the configuration index, prefixed with the system name for multiple systems):

DatasetContent
energiesenergy grid (eV)
dostotal DOS (states/eV)
pdos/<label>PDOS of every label, e.g. pdos/Pb
cohp/<A-B>COHP of every pair, e.g. cohp/Pb-Br
fermi_energyFermi energy (only with nelec)
idos, icohp/<A-B>integrated DOS and COHP on the energy grid (only with nelec)
icohp_fermi/<A-B>ICOHP at the Fermi energy (only with nelec)

The group attributes contain the method and its settings. Reading the data, e.g. in Julia:

using HDF5
h5open("dos.h5") do f
    g = f["1"]
    E = read(g["energies"]); cohp = read(g["cohp/Pb-Br"]); E_F = read(g["fermi_energy"])
end

Notes

  • KPM determines the Fermi energy from the integrated DOS. For insulators, it is placed in the middle of the widest region without states near the electron count. For small systems with discrete spectra (e.g. a small supercell at Γ), several such regions may exist within the stochastic error; use the exact method there (the default for up to 4000 orbitals).
  • With MPI, the structures are distributed over the ranks as in the standard run.

Functions

The post-processing can also be used directly, e.g. for a real-space Hamiltonian from an earlier run:

Hamster.compute_dos — Function
compute_dos(Hr, Rs, orbitals, atoms, settings=DOSSettings(); ks=zeros(3, 1), soc=false)

Compute the DOS, PDOS and COHP of a real-space Hamiltonian.

Arguments

  • Hr: Real-space Hamiltonian, one matrix per lattice vector (as returned by get_hr).
  • Rs: Lattice vectors (in units of the lattice vectors), one per column.
  • orbitals: Name of every orbital, e.g. "Pb-s" or "Br-px↑" (see orbital_labels).
  • atoms: Atom index of every orbital (used to exclude on-site terms from the COHP).
  • settings: DOSSettings.
  • ks: k-points (in units of the reciprocal lattice vectors) with equal weights.
  • soc: Whether the orbitals are spin orbitals (spin degeneracy 1 instead of 2).

Returns

A named tuple with energies, dos, pdos (energies × labels), cohp (energies × pairs), labels, pairs, method, and, if settings.nelec is set, fermi_energy, idos, icohp (integrated curves) and icohp_fermi (ICOHP at the Fermi energy).
