# Ewald Onsite Correction

An Ewald-based onsite correction is enabled when an `Ewald` block is present in the configuration file. The correction is only applied if effective charges are defined for all atomic species via the `qeff` parameter. Atoms of one species can carry different charges depending on their bonded neighbour (see [Site-Dependent Charges](#Site-Dependent-Charges)). The effective charges of a structure must add up to zero, unless its net charge is compensated: in slabs (`slab_axis`, see [Slabs](#Slabs)), clusters (`cluster`, see [Clusters](#Clusters)) or with `charge_compensation`. A net charge without compensation stops the run with a list of the site charges, since it usually points to a wrong `qeff` or an atom without the expected neighbour.

## Overview

The Ewald onsite correction introduces a physically motivated, long-range electrostatic contribution to tight-binding onsite energies.

It accounts for the electrostatic potential generated by all ions in the system and maps it onto onsite Hamiltonian terms.

The correction is of the form:

```math
\Delta \varepsilon_o = -\gamma_{s(i), l(o)} \, \phi_i
```

for the orbital ``o`` of atom ``i``, where:

- ``\phi_i`` is the electrostatic potential at atomic site ``i`` (computed via Ewald, PME, Wolf, or Zahn), optionally
  including the potential of induced dipoles (see [Induced Dipoles](#Induced-Dipoles))
- ``\gamma_{s(i), l(o)}`` is a coupling parameter: one per species (default), one in total or one per species and
  orbital type (s, p or d), see [Couplings](#Couplings)
- ``s(i)`` denotes the atomic species of atom ``i`` and ``l(o)`` the orbital type of ``o``
---

## Physical Motivation

In a full electronic structure description, the electrostatic (Hartree) potential contributes to the Hamiltonian as:

```math
\hat{H} = \hat{H}_0 - e \, \Phi(\mathbf{r})
```

Projecting onto localized orbitals $\chi_i$ gives:

```math
\Delta \varepsilon_i = -e \, \langle \chi_i \mid \Phi(\mathbf{r}) \mid \chi_i \rangle
```

In this model, we approximate:

```math
\langle \chi_i \mid \Phi(\mathbf{r}) \mid \chi_i \rangle \approx \Phi(\mathbf{R}_i)
```

which leads to a linear relationship between onsite energy shifts and site potentials.

The parameter ``\gamma`` absorbs:

- orbital averaging effects
- screening
- differences between model charges and true charge density

---

## Electrostatic Potential

The potential ``\phi_i`` is computed from effective charges ``q_i``:

```math
\phi_i = \sum_j q_j K(r_{ij})
```

where K(r) is the chosen kernel.

### Supported Methods

#### Ewald / PME (`method = "ewald"`)

- Exact electrostatics under periodic boundary conditions
- Includes:
  - real-space contribution
  - reciprocal-space contribution
  - self-interaction correction

#### Wolf / Zahn (`method = "wolf"` / `"zahn"`)

- Approximate, short-ranged electrostatics
- Uses screened Coulomb kernel:

```math
  K(r) = \mathrm{erfc}(α r) / r - \mathrm{shift}
```

- `wolf`: the shift is the kernel at the cutoff, so the potential vanishes there; `zahn` (damped shifted force)
  also subtracts the linear term, so that the force vanishes at the cutoff as well
- No reciprocal-space contribution
- Faster ``(O(N))``, but approximate

### Damping α and cutoff

With `alpha = 0` (default), α is computed from the Ewald `rcut`:

- `method = ewald`: α = 4 / rcut. Here, α only splits the sum into a real- and a reciprocal-space part; the result
  does not depend on it.
- `method = wolf` / `zahn`: α = 3 / rcut (erfc(3) ≈ 2⋅10⁻⁵ at the cutoff). Here, α is a physical parameter: each charge
  is screened by a Gaussian charge cloud of radius ≈ 1/α, so potential fluctuations with wavelengths longer than
  ≈ π/α are suppressed. Smaller α (larger rcut) comes closer to Ewald. The cost grows with rcut³.

For `method = ewald`, the default `rcut` is the TB cutoff. For Wolf and Zahn, `rcut` sets the range of the
electrostatics, and α = 3 / rcut follows from it (unless `alpha` is set, then rcut = 3 / α). In a fit without `rcut` and
`alpha`, the range follows from the training cells: rcut = 1.5 L, i.e. α = 2 / L, where L is the largest perpendicular
width of the training cells (the longest wavelength in them). Wolf then keeps at least 90 % of the potential of every wavelength
that occurs in the training data (the fraction 1 - exp(-k²/4α²) at k = 2π/L), while longer wavelengths, which only occur
in larger cells and are not tested by the training data, are screened. For 2×2×2 CsPbBr3 (L = 11.87 Å), α = 0.168 1/Å
and rcut = 17.8 Å. α and rcut are written to the parameter file at the end of the fit (see below), so that the model
uses the same screening in larger cells. Without a fit, `rcut` or `alpha` have to come from the parameter file or the
config.

## Couplings

How many couplings the model has is set by `coupling` in the `Ewald` block:

| `coupling` | couplings | set with |
|---|---|---|
| `global` | one for all species and orbitals | `charge_scale` of the `Ewald` block |
| `species` (default) | one per species | `charge_scale` of the atom block (default: of the `Ewald` block) |
| `orbital` | one per species and orbital type (s, p, d) | `charge_scale_s`, `charge_scale_p`, `charge_scale_d` of the atom block (default: its `charge_scale`) |

With `coupling = global`, the couplings describe one screening of the potential for all orbitals, the most constrained
choice. Separate couplings per orbital type describe that orbitals of different extent average the potential over
different regions: a diffuse orbital sees a smaller part of the potential at its nucleus than a compact one. Tags that
the chosen mode does not use stop the run (`charge_scale` in an atom block with `global`, `charge_scale_<l>` without
`orbital`). For example, a hyperparameter search over `Pb_charge_scale`, or a smaller coupling of the Pb p orbitals:

```bash
begin Ewald
    charge_scale = 0.6
    coupling = orbital
end

begin atom Pb
    qeff = 1.248
    charge_scale = 0.4      # γ of the Pb s orbitals, the other species use 0.6
    charge_scale_p = 0.2    # γ of the Pb p orbitals
end
```

With `update = true` in the `Ewald` block, the couplings are fitted together with the other models, e.g. with
`coupling = orbital` using the gradient
``\partial L / \partial \gamma_{s,l} = -\sum_{i \in s} \sum_{o \in i,\, l(o) = l} \mathrm{Re}\,(\partial L / \partial H_{R=0})_{oo}\, \phi_i``.
The couplings are written to `params.dat` at the end of a fit after the TB and SOC parameters as `ewald_<element>`
lines (with `global` the same value for every species), and with `coupling = orbital` as `ewald_<element>_<l>` lines
(`<l>` = s, p or d, e.g. `ewald_Pb_s`, `ewald_Pb_p`) where the orbital types of a species differ. They are read with
`init_params = params.dat` in the `Ewald` block, which takes precedence over the atom blocks; an `ewald_<element>`
line sets all orbital types of the species. A file with couplings per orbital type needs `coupling = orbital`, and
`coupling = global` needs the same coupling for all species in the file.
Without `init_params`, the `ewald_` lines of a parameter file are ignored, so that an explicitly set `charge_scale`
(e.g. in a hyperparameter search) is not overwritten. The effective charges `qeff` and thus the
electrostatic potential (and its neutrality) are not changed by the coupling.

## Site-Dependent Charges

With one `qeff` per species, all atoms of a species carry the same charge. This is a poor description when a species
occurs in chemically different sites, e.g. the H atoms of methylammonium (CH₃NH₃⁺): the H atoms of the NH₃ group carry a
much larger positive charge than those of the CH₃ group, and averaging them moves the positive charge of the molecule
to the wrong end. Charges per site are set with `qeff_<element>` in the atom block, where `<element>` is the bonded
neighbour:

```bash
begin atom H
    qeff = 0.239      # fallback for H atoms without a C or N neighbour
    qeff_N = 0.349    # H bonded to N
    qeff_C = 0.120    # H bonded to C
end
```

The bonded neighbour of an atom is its nearest neighbour among the elements of its `qeff_<element>` keys within
`site_rcut` of the `Ewald` block (default 2 Å, at most `rcut`). It is determined for every structure, from the positions
used for the potentials, on a neighbour grid (linear in the number of atoms). Atoms without such a neighbour keep `qeff`.

The charges of each structure must add up to zero, also with site-dependent charges. The charges per site are typically
averages of charges from DFT (e.g. Hirshfeld charges) and have to be adjusted, e.g. in the last digit of one species,
so that the formula unit is neutral. If a structure is not neutral, Hamster stops and lists the charges of all sites.
With site-dependent charges, this also catches unexpected bonding, e.g. an H atom that is not bonded to C or N and
keeps `qeff`. The sites found and their charges are listed in the Ewald model summary in `hamster.out`, e.g.
`H(N) 0.349 (24)`. The couplings do not depend on the sites.

## Induced Dipoles

Fixed effective charges miss the polarization of the electron clouds: an ion in the field of its surroundings
develops a dipole, whose potential changes the on-site energies of its neighbours. For soft, polarizable ions (e.g.
halides and Pb²⁺ in halide perovskites) this part of the potential is correlated over several neighbour shells, follows
the field of molecular dipoles and other ions rather than the local bond geometry, and is not captured by the point
charges or local ML descriptors. The tag `polarizability` (Å³) in an atom block makes the species polarizable:

```bash
begin Ewald
    method = wolf
    alpha = 0.3
end

begin atom Br
    qeff = -0.541
    polarizability = 1.5
end
```

Every atom ``j`` of a polarizable species ``t`` gets the first-order induced dipole
``\boldsymbol{\mu}_j = p_t \mathbf{E}_j / k_e`` in the field ``\mathbf{E}_j`` of the effective charges, and the
potential of these dipoles is added to ``\phi_i``:

```math
\phi_i \rightarrow \phi_i + \sum_t p_t \, \Phi_{i,t}, \qquad
\Phi_{i,t} = \sum_{j \in t} f(r_{ij}) \, \mathbf{E}_j \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{ji} \Big|_{p_t = 1}
```

Field and dipole potential use the radial derivative ``f = -\mathrm{d}K/\mathrm{d}r`` of the Wolf or Zahn kernel
(the field of a unit charge) within `rcut`, on the neighbour grid of the potential (linear in the number of atoms). The
dipoles respond only to the charges, not to each other (first order), so the potential is linear in the
polarizabilities: the dipole potentials ``\Phi`` are computed once per structure and centered per species like
``\phi``. Induced dipoles need `method = wolf` or `zahn` and are not available for slabs and clusters.

Induced dipoles are only used if a species has the tag `polarizability`. The polarizabilities are fixed unless
`update_polarizability = true` is set in the `Ewald` block (independent of `update`, which fits the couplings). They are
written to `params.dat` as `polarizability_<element>` lines and read with `init_params` of the `Ewald` block. Species
with the tag `polarizability` (also `polarizability = 0`, e.g. to fit it from zero) or a
`polarizability_<element>` line in that file are polarizable. A good starting point are polarizabilities fitted to the
electrostatic potentials at the nuclei of DFT calculations (VASP: "average (electrostatic) potential at core" in the
OUTCAR), together with fixed couplings.

## Settings in the Parameter File

At the end of a fit, the settings that define the potentials are written to the header of `params.dat`, together
with the couplings and polarizabilities:

```
begin unknown_system
  rcut = 5.5
  ...
  ewald_method = wolf
  ewald_rcut = 18.0
  ewald_alpha = 0.16666666666666666
  ewald_qeff_Br = -0.756
  ewald_qeff_Cs = 1.02
  ewald_qeff_Pb = 1.248
end
```

Site-dependent charges are written as `ewald_qeff_<species>_<element>`, e.g. `ewald_qeff_H_N = 0.349` for `qeff_N` in
the block of H.

When a model is built from this file (`init_params` of the `Ewald` block, otherwise `init_params`), settings that are
not set in the config are taken from the header, so that a fitted model is used with the settings it was fitted with,
e.g., in larger cells. The adopted settings are printed and appear in the model summary. If a setting is set in the
config and differs from the header, Hamster stops with an error, since the fitted couplings belong to the settings of
the file. To change them deliberately (e.g., to refit with a different α), set `check_params = false` in the `Ewald`
block. For `method = ewald`, `rcut` and `alpha` only affect the numerics and are not taken from the file; `method` and
`qeff` are.

## Mean Centering

The electrostatic potential is only defined up to a constant.

To ensure consistency with onsite fitting, we subtract a per-species mean:

```math
\phi_i \rightarrow \phi_i - \langle \phi \rangle _ {s(i)}
```

This ensures:

- no arbitrary offset in onsite energies
- compatibility across different structures

`centering` in the `Ewald` block sets which mean is subtracted:

- `global` (default without `slab_axis`): the mean of each species over all structures of a run. Changes of the
  species means between structures are kept, but the onsite corrections of a structure depend slightly on the other
  structures computed together (``\delta\bar\phi`` in the model summary).
- `structure` (default with `slab_axis`): the means of each structure. The onsite corrections of a structure no longer
  depend on the run, and a constant potential offset that differs between structures drops out. With species-dependent
  couplings ``\gamma_s``, such an offset would shift the onsite energies of the species against each other; for slabs,
  the level of the plane-averaged potential depends on the configuration of the whole slab. In return, a change of the
  mean potential of one species relative to another between structures is removed as well.

```
    φ̄: species mean of the site potentials over all structures of this run.
    σ: width of Δε; max/σ: largest |Δε| relative to σ; >3σ %: fraction of
      sites with |Δε| > 3σ.
    σ_strc: spread of the per-structure species means, i.e. the part of Δε
      that is lost if the model is built for a single structure.
    δφ̄: uncertainty of φ̄ in meV of Δε, σ_strc / √(number of structures)
      (single structure: σ / √sites). The onsite corrections shift by about
      δφ̄ if other structures are computed together.
================================================================================
```

- `couplings γ` lists the couplings (`all`, per species or per species and orbital type, see `coupling`; marked
  `fitted` with `update = true`), and the table has one row per coupling. With induced dipoles, a line
  `polarizabilities (Å³)` follows, and φ in the table includes the dipole potential.
- `φ̄` is the species mean that is subtracted (see [Mean Centering](#Mean-Centering)). It is taken over the structures
  of the current run, so it can differ between training, validation and later runs.
- `σ`, `min`, `max` and the 1% / 99% percentiles describe the distribution of the corrections. `max/σ` and `>3σ %`
  show how pronounced the tails are, which is useful to compare large cells with the training cells.
- `σ_strc` is the spread of the per-structure species means. This part of the correction is lost if the model is built
  for a single structure.
- `δφ̄` is the uncertainty of `φ̄`: the corrections of a structure shift by about this amount if it is computed together
  with other structures. If it exceeds 10 meV for a species, the summary ends with a warning (also printed to the log).
  It decreases with the number of structures in the run and with the cell size: e.g., for 2x2x2 CsPbBr3 cells (Wolf)
  10 meV with 10 structures, 3 meV with 100 (the band gaps of the two runs differ by about 3 meV) and 30-50 meV for a
  single structure, while the estimate for a single 16x16x16 cell is 1-2 meV.

## Slabs

In a slab (a cell with vacuum along one lattice vector), aligned dipoles of successive layers (e.g. alternating bond
lengths along the surface normal) add up to a potential step across the slab, which shifts the levels of the surface
layers relative to the interior. This is a long-range effect: the potential of a charged plane does not decay with
distance. Wolf and Zahn keep only the short-range part ``\mathrm{erfc}(\alpha r)/r`` of the Coulomb interaction and
miss it, while the full Ewald sum treats the slab as periodic along the normal, so that the dipole of the slab creates
an artificial field across the vacuum.

With `slab_axis` (1, 2 or 3, the lattice vector across the vacuum; default 0: periodic) in the `Ewald` block:

- **Wolf and Zahn**: the plane-averaged part of the missing long range is added. The average of the smooth part
  ``\mathrm{erf}(\alpha r)/r`` over the planes parallel to the surface is the potential of Gaussian charge sheets
  (the ``k_\parallel = 0`` component of the Ewald sum),

  ```math
  \bar\phi(z) = -\frac{2\pi}{A} \sum_j q_j \left[ u_j \,\mathrm{erf}(\alpha u_j) + \frac{e^{-\alpha^2 u_j^2}}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}} \right],
  \qquad u_j = z - z_j,
  ```

  with the heights ``z_j`` of the atoms along the normal and the area ``A`` of the cell in the plane of the slab. It uses
  the same α as the Wolf/Zahn sum, so nothing is counted twice, and it describes an isolated slab (no field in the
  vacuum, i.e. it includes the dipole correction). The components that vary within the planes decay like
  ``\exp(-G^2/4\alpha^2)`` with the in-plane reciprocal lattice vectors ``G`` and are left out.
- **Ewald**: the dipole correction of the slab (Yeh and Berkowitz) is added,
  ``\phi_i \rightarrow \phi_i + 4\pi M z_i / V`` with the dipole moment ``M = \sum_j q_j z_j`` along the normal.

The heights are measured from the vacuum: the atoms are placed so that the largest gap between them along the axis
(the vacuum) lies at the cell boundary, so a slab may cross the periodic boundary. The potentials enter the onsite
energies through the couplings ``\gamma_s`` as before. The model summary states the slab treatment.

The long-range term is screened by the electrons like a macroscopic field (by about ``1/\varepsilon_\infty``), which can
differ from the screening that the couplings ``\gamma_s`` absorb for the short-range part. `slab_scale` (default 1)
multiplies the slab term (the plane-averaged potential, or the dipole correction for `method = ewald`):
``\phi_i = \phi_i^\mathrm{short} + \lambda\, \bar\phi_i``.

```bash
begin Ewald
    method = wolf
    alpha = 0.2
    slab_axis = 3                   # vacuum along a3
    slab_scale = 0.4                # screening of the long-range term (optional)
    charge_compensation = surface   # default with slab_axis
    centering = structure           # default with slab_axis (see Mean Centering)
end
```

`slab_axis` applies to all structures of a run; use it only for runs with slabs.

### Charge compensation

The slab terms need a neutral slab, otherwise the potential rises linearly into the vacuum. Effective charges need not
add up to zero: in a non-stoichiometric slab, or whenever the effective charges of the atomic layers do not cancel. In
CsPbBr₃ (001), for example, the PbBr₂ and CsBr layers are neutral with formal charges, but with effective charges
(``q_\mathrm{Pb} \neq -2 q_\mathrm{Br}``) they carry charges ``\mp\sigma``, and a slab terminated by PbBr₂ on both
sides has a net charge. Where the compensating charge sits matters for the long range, so `charge_compensation` in the
`Ewald` block sets it:

- `global`: a uniform background on all atoms (``q_j - \bar q`` in the slab terms). The surfaces of a slab with charged
  layers then stay charged, with a potential step between surface and interior that the electrons would screen.
- `surface` (default with `slab_axis`): the net charge is placed on the outermost atomic layer of each side and split so
  that the plane-averaged field between them vanishes on average, as the electrons do at a real surface. For a slab
  with layers of charge ``\pm\sigma`` and the same termination on both sides, each surface layer takes half of the
  layer charge; for a polar stacking, the surface charge of the bulk polarization. Layers are separated by gaps of more
  than 1 Å along the axis.
- `local`: the net charge is distributed over the atoms with fewer neighbors of opposite charge than the most common
  number of their species, in proportion to the missing neighbors, e.g. on the cations around an anion vacancy. Meant
  for defects; the neighbors follow from the first shell of every pair of species.

`global` is the default without `slab_axis`. The compensating charges of `surface` stand for a smooth electronic
charge at the surfaces and enter only the slab terms; the effective charges of the surface atoms in the short-range
Wolf/Zahn/Ewald sum stay unchanged. Those of `local` sit on the atoms next to the defect and are added to the effective
charges in the whole sum. Both are determined from the reference positions of each structure, so that they are the
same for all of its distortions.

## Clusters

An isolated cluster, e.g. a quantum dot, is computed in a box with vacuum in all directions. The full Ewald sum treats
the box as periodic with tinfoil boundary conditions: the dipole moments of the periodic images of the cluster add up
to a uniform field across the cluster, which shifts the potentials of its two sides against each other. The field
decreases with the volume of the box, ``\propto M/V``, so it matters for polar clusters in small boxes.

With `cluster = true` in the `Ewald` block (only for `method = ewald`), the dipole correction in all three directions
is added (vacuum instead of tinfoil boundary conditions),

```math
\phi_i \rightarrow \phi_i + \frac{4\pi}{3V}\, \mathbf{M} \cdot (\mathbf{r}_i - \mathbf{c}),
\qquad \mathbf{M} = \sum_j q_j (\mathbf{r}_j - \mathbf{c}),
```

with the center ``\mathbf{c}`` of the atoms. The positions are unwrapped first: along every lattice vector, the largest
gap between atoms (the vacuum) is moved to the cell boundary, so a cluster may cross the periodic boundary. The factor
1/3 is the depolarization factor of a cube. For a cubic box, the images then only enter through their quadrupole
moments, a constant that the mean centering removes, and higher multipoles, which decay with the box size like
``1/L^5``. For a box far from cubic, the correction is only approximate; use a cubic box with enough vacuum.

Wolf and Zahn need no correction: they sum the neighbors within `rcut`, and with `rcut` larger than the cluster (and a
vacuum wider than `rcut`) that is the potential of the isolated cluster. They are an alternative for small clusters;
for large clusters, a cutoff of the cluster size makes them expensive, and `method = ewald` with `cluster` is the
better choice.

The effective charges of a non-stoichiometric cluster need not add up to zero, e.g. a CsPbBr₃ dot with excess CsBr
on the surface (``q_\mathrm{Cs} + q_\mathrm{Br} \neq 0``). With `cluster`, `charge_compensation = global` (the
default) places the uniform background on the atoms of the cluster (``q_j - \bar q``) in the whole sum, so that the
Ewald sum of the box is neutral. `local` compensation is meant for defects in a bulk-like structure and is not
suited for clusters: their most common coordination is that of the surface. With `cluster`, the species means are
subtracted per structure by default (see [Mean Centering](#Mean-Centering)).

```bash
begin Ewald
    method = ewald
    rcut = 10.0
    cluster = true                  # dipole correction of the images
    charge_compensation = global    # default: uniform background on the atoms
    centering = structure           # default with cluster
end
```

## Real-Space Neighbor Grid

The real-space Ewald sum reuses the neighbor grid and translation vectors of the structure, which cover pairs up to
the TB cutoff `rcut` and the `grid_size`. If the Ewald `rcut` is larger, both are rebuilt for the Ewald cutoff, and the
model summary states `real-space neighbor grid: rebuilt`. The range of the rebuilt translation vectors follows from the
cutoff over the height of the cell perpendicular to each pair of lattice vectors, plus the extent of the atoms, so that
cells with a short period in one direction, such as slabs with a small in-plane cell, are summed completely.
